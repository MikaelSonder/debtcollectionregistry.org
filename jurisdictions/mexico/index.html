EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Debt collection in Mexico is a formal, document-driven recovery function combining commercial negotiation, executive proceedings, ordinary commercial litigation, precautionary measures and court-led execution. The most important procedural distinction is between claims supported by a título ejecutivo — an instrument that carries its own enforceability under law — and claims that require the creditor first to prove the existence and amount of the debt in ordinary proceedings. A promissory note (pagaré), cheque, bill of exchange, notarised debt acknowledgement, final arbitral award or other qualifying instrument can give the creditor access to the juicio ejecutivo mercantil, the principal summary commercial recovery procedure under Articles 1391–1414 of the Código de Comercio.

The executive-commercial route is operationally powerful because the court can require payment and order embargo — attachment of sufficient debtor assets — at the outset of the action if payment is not made. The process is not designed to decide an uncertain claim from scratch; it is intended to enforce a debt evidenced by an instrument with statutory executive force. The debtor can oppose and defend, but the early attachment protects the creditor's recovery position. If the creditor lacks a qualifying instrument or the matter is genuinely disputed, the ordinary commercial procedure is used. For international creditors, Mexico therefore requires disciplined original-document management, Spanish-language procedural handling, correct state and court selection, and practical knowledge of debtor assets.

PRIMARY OUTCOME

Lawful recovery of overdue claims in Mexico through negotiated resolution, juicio ejecutivo mercantil, ordinary commercial litigation, court-authorised embargo, and execution against Mexican debtor assets.

COUNTRY CHARACTERISTICS
LEGAL CULTURECodified, formal, document-driven, and federal in commercial law but operationally dependent on state and federal court structures. Mexican recovery practice values original enforceable instruments, clear debtor identity, Spanish-language evidence, proper forum selection, early embargo analysis and realistic knowledge of debtor assets.
ENFORCEMENT MODELCommercial enforcement is court-led. In a juicio ejecutivo mercantil, a qualifying título ejecutivo permits the court to require payment and order embargo of sufficient assets from the outset. Statutory asset order prioritises goods, readily collectable receivables, other movables, real estate, and then further rights or actions. Court officers execute service and attachment under judicial authority.
LICENSING ENVIRONMENTMexico does not operate a single nationwide debt-collection-agency licence regime for ordinary commercial claims. Lawyers act within the applicable professional framework. Private collection providers cannot seize assets or use coercion; embargo, sale and judicial execution are reserved to the competent court and authorised officers.
DATA PROTECTIONDebt recovery files may include personal, financial and commercially sensitive information. Processing is governed by the Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties and related rules. The data-protection institutional landscape is evolving; creditors should confirm the competent authority and applicable federal or local framework before processing, sharing or transferring debtor data.
LANGUAGE EXPECTATIONSpanish is the operative language for Mexican court filings, executive proceedings, embargo orders, service and judicial administration. English-language contracts are common in cross-border transactions, but Spanish translations and locally admissible evidence are generally required for litigation and enforcement.
KEY AUTHORITIES
CÓDIGO DE COMERCIOCore federal source for commercial obligations and commercial procedure. Articles 1391–1414 regulate juicio ejecutivo mercantil, the expedited commercial route for claims supported by a document that carries executive force.
JUICIO EJECUTIVO MERCANTILSummary commercial proceeding available where the creditor holds a título ejecutivo and the debt is certain, liquid and due. It combines payment demand with an immediate judicial embargo order if the debtor does not pay at service.
COMPETENT MEXICAN COURTSFederal and state courts exercise commercial jurisdiction according to the applicable legal framework, territorial jurisdiction, parties and subject matter. They determine commercial claims, authorise precautionary measures and direct embargo and execution.
COURT OFFICERS / ACTUARIOSAuthorised judicial officers serve process, carry out embargo and record enforcement acts under the court's direction. Private agencies cannot perform these coercive functions.
PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION FRAMEWORKMexico's personal-data protection regime applies to private-sector debtor information. The institutional authority structure is changing, so creditors and collection providers should verify current competent supervision and comply with notice, purpose, security and rights obligations.
TYPICAL TIMELINE
STAGE 1Invoice is issued and the contractual due date passes.
STAGE 2Account reconciliation, formal demand, contract review, and assessment of Mexican debtor identity, original documents, evidence, assets, and forum are initiated.
STAGE 3Commercial negotiation, settlement discussion, and evaluation of título ejecutivo status, executive procedure, ordinary litigation, arbitration or insolvency options are undertaken.
STAGE 4Where a qualifying executive title exists, the creditor files juicio ejecutivo mercantil with the original instrument and required supporting documentation before the competent court.
STAGE 5The judge issues an order requiring the debtor to pay. If payment is not made, the court officer embargos sufficient assets to secure the debt and costs, after which the debtor may raise defences within the procedural framework.
STAGE 6The claim proceeds through defence, evidence, oral hearing or other applicable commercial procedure; if the creditor succeeds, the court issues the corresponding enforcement and sale directions.
STAGE 7Attached assets are preserved, valued, sold, collected, applied toward the judgment, or released in accordance with the court order and statutory priority rules.
TYPICAL TIMEFRAMES
REMINDER PHASEMay begin immediately after default. A Spanish-language demand should identify the contractual basis, principal, interest, evidence, payment deadline, and appropriate intended escalation route.
COLLECTION PHASEOften takes weeks to several months depending on debtor responsiveness, commercial relationship, documentary quality, settlement prospects, title availability, and asset intelligence.
DISPUTE REVIEWCan arise immediately where the debtor disputes liability, amount, delivery, quality, performance, set-off, forum, governing law, or the status of the alleged executive title. A genuine dispute may require ordinary commercial litigation.
JUICIO EJECUTIVO MERCANTILThe procedure is designed to secure a claim evidenced by a título ejecutivo. The initial order combines a demand for payment with an embargo direction if payment is not made. Subsequent timing depends on service, debtor defences, evidence, hearings, court workload, asset location and appeals.
ORDINARY COMMERCIAL LITIGATIONUsed when the creditor lacks a qualifying title or the dispute requires full judicial determination. It generally takes longer because the court must determine the existence and amount of the debt before final enforcement.
ENFORCEMENTExecution timing depends on the assets attached, third-party cooperation, valuation, objections, priority claims, sale processes and the practical ability to convert debtor assets into payment.
CROSS-BORDER RELEVANCE

Mexico is one of the Americas' most significant cross-border recovery jurisdictions, deeply integrated with the United States, Canada, Latin America, Europe and Asia through manufacturing, automotive production, nearshoring, logistics, agriculture, energy, technology, trade and services. Mexico is outside the EU civil-justice system and does not apply Brussels I (recast) or the European Enforcement Order. Foreign creditors must conduct tailored Mexican analysis of jurisdiction, choice of law, arbitration clauses, state location, Spanish translations, original documentary evidence, debtor assets and the recognition route for any existing foreign judgment or award. Mexico's participation in the New York Convention makes arbitral-award enforcement a significant cross-border option.

Example: a German automotive supplier delivers components to a Mexican manufacturer and the buyer fails to pay a final invoice supported by a signed promissory note. The supplier reviews the original note, delivery evidence, Mexican entity registration, the contract's dispute-resolution clause and assets in the relevant state. A formal demand may produce settlement. If it does not, the promissory note can support juicio ejecutivo mercantil, allowing a court order for payment and, if unpaid, embargo at the beginning of the action. If the debt is genuinely disputed or lacks a qualifying title, the supplier may instead need ordinary commercial proceedings or the agreed arbitration route before final execution.

OPERATING CONSTRAINTS
APPLICABLE LAWCódigo de Comercio • Federal Civil Code • General Law on Negotiable Instruments and Credit Operations • Federal Code of Civil Procedure and relevant state procedural law • Arbitration framework • Bankruptcy and commercial insolvency law • Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties • applicable international treaties including the New York Convention.
DEBTOR RIGHTSDebtors have rights to proper court service, notice of payment demand and embargo, procedural defences, evidence, appeal, and judicial remedies. Embargo must be court-authorised and restricted to assets legally available for attachment. Private collectors may not exercise coercion, seize assets or bypass court procedure.
DATA PROTECTIONPersonal, financial and corporate information must be handled lawfully, accurately, securely and proportionately under Mexican private-sector data-protection law. Collection files require clear purpose, privacy notice compliance, controlled access, data minimisation, retention discipline, confidentiality and safeguards for international transfers or disclosures to lawyers, courts and authorised partners.
LICENSING REQUIREMENTSNo single nationwide collection-agency licence governs ordinary commercial B2B recovery. Lawyers act under the applicable professional framework. Judicial embargo, service and execution are court functions performed by authorised officers. Sector-specific or consumer-credit restrictions may apply separately and should be reviewed where relevant.
PROCEDURAL LIMITSJuicio ejecutivo mercantil requires a qualifying título ejecutivo and a debt that is certain, liquid and due. Original documentary instruments are often critical. A claim lacking executive force or involving genuine factual disputes normally requires ordinary commercial litigation or arbitration. Foreign titles require recognition analysis, correct court jurisdiction, Spanish translations, legalisation or apostille where relevant, and compliance with Mexican public policy and due-process standards.

PROCESS FLOW
1. TRIGGERAn unpaid invoice, matured receivable, promissory note or silent Mexican debtor enters the recovery workflow.
2. VALIDATIONThe file is checked for contractual basis, debt maturity, Mexican debtor identity, original evidence, título ejecutivo status, forum, limitation, dispute profile and accessible assets.
3. NOTICEA formal demand is issued in the appropriate language, identifying the principal, interest, legal basis, payment deadline and intended escalation route.
4. CONTACTCommercial communication and negotiation are conducted to encourage payment, identify defences and assess a documented settlement or payment arrangement.
5. ARRANGEMENTWhere commercially justified, a payment plan, settlement agreement, debt acknowledgement, security enhancement or documented compromise is evaluated.

TYPICAL QUESTIONS
CAN PAYMENT BE ENFORCED?Yes. A creditor with a qualifying título ejecutivo may bring juicio ejecutivo mercantil. The court can require payment and order embargo of sufficient assets at the outset if payment is not made. Other claims can be enforced after ordinary proceedings and a final judgment.
CAN A MEXICAN LAWYER RECOVER THE CLAIM?Yes. Mexican lawyers can manage demands, settlement, executive proceedings, ordinary commercial litigation, precautionary measures, embargo and execution, arbitration, insolvency and cross-border recovery work.
DOES COLLECTION REQUIRE AUTHORISATION?No single nationwide collection-agency licence applies to ordinary B2B recovery. Lawyers act under the applicable professional framework. Judicial embargo and execution powers are reserved to courts and their authorised officers.
CAN A FOREIGN CREDITOR RECOVER A DEBT IN MEXICO?Yes. Foreign creditors may pursue voluntary recovery, juicio ejecutivo mercantil with a qualifying instrument, ordinary commercial litigation, arbitration, recognition of qualifying foreign judgments or awards, and enforcement against Mexican assets.
WHAT IS THE TYPICAL TIMELINE?Voluntary recovery may start immediately after default. A properly supported executive action can produce a payment demand and embargo direction at the start, but defended litigation and asset sale timing vary materially by court, state, service, evidence, assets and appeals.
WHICH AUTHORITY HANDLES ENFORCEMENT?Competent Mexican courts handle commercial enforcement, while authorised court officers execute service and embargo. Federal commercial law applies nationwide, but the practical court and enforcement system is administered through federal and state jurisdictions.
MEXICO COLLECTION MODEL
MEXICO MODELMexico combines commercially pragmatic voluntary recovery, an unusually powerful juicio ejecutivo mercantil for claims backed by títulos ejecutivos, ordinary commercial litigation for disputed or non-executive claims, and court-led embargo and asset sale. The model rewards original-document control, Spanish-language procedural accuracy, correct state-level forum choice, and early asset intelligence.
INTERNATIONAL POSITIONMexico is a core Americas recovery jurisdiction for North American supply chains, nearshoring, manufacturing, logistics, technology, agriculture and trade. It is outside the EU civil-justice system, requiring tailored Mexican analysis of jurisdiction, title enforceability, original documents, Spanish translations, arbitration, foreign judgments, asset location and judicial embargo. New York Convention award enforcement is particularly relevant.
