EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Debt collection in Slovakia is a structured civil-law recovery function combining voluntary collection, payment orders, a centralised electronic debt-collection route, ordinary civil litigation, and a nationally centralised enforcement system. For certain and undisputed monetary claims, creditors can seek a payment order under the Civil Litigation Procedure Code. The debtor has 15 days from delivery to pay or file a defence. Slovakia also operates a specialised electronic debt-collection procedure under Act No. 307/2016 Coll., designed for documented monetary claims submitted through a standard electronic form with an authorised electronic signature.

Slovakia's enforcement architecture is unusually centralised. Under Act No. 233/1995 Coll., the Enforcement Code, only an enforceable title can support enforcement. The enforcement petition is filed electronically with District Court Banská Bystrica, the single enforcement court with nationwide territorial jurisdiction, and the court assigns a state-appointed enforcement agent (súdny exekútor) to carry out the measures. For foreign B2B creditors, this creates a defined progression from evidence and payment order to centralised electronic filing and agent-led execution.

PRIMARY OUTCOME

Lawful recovery of overdue claims in Slovakia through amicable resolution, payment orders, electronic debt collection, civil litigation, and compulsory enforcement by a court enforcement agent.

COUNTRY CHARACTERISTICS
LEGAL CULTUREFormal, codified, and document-driven. Slovak recovery practice rewards accurate claim documentation, proper debtor identification, procedural compliance, and early selection of the payment-order, electronic, or ordinary litigation route. Slovak is the operative language for formal domestic procedure.
ENFORCEMENT MODELCompulsory enforcement is an exercise of public power carried out by a state-appointed and authorised court enforcement agent (súdny exekútor). Enforcement petitions are lodged electronically with District Court Banská Bystrica, which is the single enforcement court for the whole Slovak Republic and assigns the agent.
LICENSING ENVIRONMENTOrdinary amicable commercial recovery is not governed by a general national collector-authorisation regime. Lawyers act under the legal-profession framework. Compulsory enforcement is reserved exclusively to court enforcement agents appointed and authorised by the state under the Enforcement Code.
DATA PROTECTIONDebt collection files involving personal and financial data are subject to GDPR and Slovak data-protection law. Lawful basis, data minimisation, secure processing, confidentiality, and carefully controlled cross-border data transfers are essential in debtor tracing, communication, and enforcement preparation.
LANGUAGE EXPECTATIONSlovak is the operative language for domestic court procedure, electronic filings, payment orders, and enforcement administration. English may be used in commercial correspondence, but legal escalation normally requires Slovak procedural documents and qualified local handling.
KEY AUTHORITIES
CIVIL LITIGATION PROCEDURE CODE (ACT NO. 160/2015 COLL.)Core framework for civil proceedings and Slovak payment orders. A creditor with a certain and undisputed debt may seek a payment order; the debtor is generally given 15 days from delivery to pay or submit a defence.
ACT NO. 307/2016 COLL. ON ELECTRONIC DEBT COLLECTIONEstablishes the specialised electronic debt-collection procedure for documented monetary claims. Applications are made through a standard electronic form and the centralised procedure is assigned to District Court Banská Bystrica.
ENFORCEMENT CODE (ACT NO. 233/1995 COLL.)Governs enforcement agents and enforcement proceedings. It requires an enforceable title, an enforcement petition, and the relevant court fee before compulsory enforcement can proceed.
DISTRICT COURT BANSKÁ BYSTRICAThe sole enforcement court with territorial jurisdiction for the whole Slovak Republic. Enforcement petitions are lodged electronically, after which the court assigns the enforcement agent under the statutory allocation system.
OFFICE FOR PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION OF THE SLOVAK REPUBLICNational data-protection supervisory authority relevant to GDPR compliance in debtor communication, debt-file processing, data access, and cross-border transfers.
TYPICAL TIMELINE
STAGE 1Invoice is issued and the due date passes.
STAGE 2A pre-litigation demand (predžalobná výzva), reminder, and evidence review are initiated.
STAGE 3The claim is prepared for voluntary settlement, a standard payment order, electronic debt collection, or ordinary litigation.
STAGE 4For a documented monetary claim, an electronic payment-order application may be filed with the centralised court through the statutory electronic form.
STAGE 5The debtor has 15 days from delivery of a payment order to pay or file a defence. A defence or unsuccessful service can move the matter into regular court proceedings.
STAGE 6Once an enforceable title exists, an electronic enforcement petition is filed with District Court Banská Bystrica and the court assigns a court enforcement agent.
STAGE 7The enforcement agent carries out attachment of bank accounts, income, movable property, securities, shares, or real estate and may arrange the sale of seized property as permitted by law.
TYPICAL TIMEFRAMES
REMINDER PHASEMay begin immediately after default. A clear pre-litigation demand supports later procedural strategy and provides a final opportunity for voluntary settlement.
COLLECTION PHASEOften takes days to several months depending on debtor responsiveness, documentary quality, commercial discussions, and the creditor's decision on whether to escalate.
DISPUTE REVIEWCan arise immediately where the debtor disputes liability, amount, delivery, or contractual terms. A contested claim generally requires the fuller procedural path rather than a streamlined payment-order resolution.
PAYMENT ORDER / ELECTRONIC DEBT COLLECTIONThe payment-order framework requires the debtor to pay or defend within 15 days of delivery. The electronic route is designed as a simplified, centralised process for claims evidenced by documents, but effective service remains essential.
LEGAL ESCALATIONOrdinary civil litigation takes longer than a payment-order route and depends on court workload, evidence, representation, service, expert issues, and appeal activity.
ENFORCEMENTOnce an enforceable title exists, the petition is filed electronically with the central enforcement court. Execution time depends on assigned-agent action, asset type, debtor cooperation, objections, and practical asset availability.
CROSS-BORDER RELEVANCE

Slovakia is a full participant in the EU civil-justice framework and is commercially important for Central European manufacturing, automotive supply chains, logistics, electronics, wholesale trade, and services. Under the Brussels I Regulation (recast), judgments from other EU Member States are recognised and enforceable in Slovakia without intermediate exequatur. Foreign creditors may also assess the European Enforcement Order for uncontested claims, the European Order for Payment, and the European Small Claims Procedure where the respective conditions are met.

Example: an Austrian manufacturer supplies components to a Slovak automotive subcontractor, the invoice becomes overdue, and the debtor does not respond. The creditor first reviews the contract, invoice, delivery documents, jurisdiction, Slovak debtor details, and potential assets. A pre-litigation demand can lead to settlement. If the claim is well documented and expected to remain undisputed, the creditor may use the Slovak payment-order or electronic debt-collection path. Once an enforceable title is obtained, the enforcement petition is filed electronically with District Court Banská Bystrica, which assigns a court enforcement agent to pursue Slovak accounts, receivables, income, movables, or real estate.

DEBTOR RIGHTSDebtors have rights to proper service, a 15-day period to pay or defend a payment order, access to ordinary proceedings for contested claims, objections and remedies in enforcement, and statutory protections applicable to income and assets. Collection communications must remain lawful and proportionate.
DATA PROTECTIONPersonal and financial data must be processed lawfully, securely, and proportionately under GDPR and Slovak law. Debt recovery files require controlled access, data minimisation, retention discipline, and appropriate safeguards for cross-border transfers or disclosure to professional partners.
LICENSING REQUIREMENTSNo general national debt-collection authorisation model governs ordinary amicable commercial recovery. Lawyers act under the legal-profession framework. Compulsory enforcement is exclusively reserved to court enforcement agents who are appointed and authorised by the state under the Enforcement Code.
PROCEDURAL LIMITSPayment-order and electronic debt-collection procedures are suited to documented monetary claims and can be disrupted by a defence or failure of effective service. Enforcement requires a valid enforceable title, an electronic petition to the central court, and the required court fee. Cross-border matters must comply with jurisdiction, choice-of-law, translation, and EU recognition rules.

PROCESS FLOW
1. TRIGGERAn unpaid invoice, matured receivable, or silent Slovak debtor enters the recovery workflow.
2. VALIDATIONThe claim is checked for maturity, debtor identity, documentary sufficiency, jurisdiction, limitation status, dispute profile, and suitability for payment order, electronic procedure, or litigation.
3. NOTICEA pre-litigation demand or formal reminder is issued, identifying the principal, interest, basis of claim, payment deadline, and prospective escalation.
4. CONTACTDebtor communication and commercial clarification are conducted to encourage voluntary payment and identify any substantive defence.
5. ARRANGEMENTWhere commercially justified, a payment plan, settlement, security arrangement, or documented compromise is evaluated.
6. ESCALATIONUnresolved claims proceed to a payment order, electronic debt collection, ordinary litigation, or an EU cross-border route as appropriate.
7. CLOSEThe claim is paid, settled, titled, enforced, transferred, or closed with a preserved evidence package for further Slovak or foreign action.

MARKET CONTEXT
MARKET SCALESlovakia is a highly integrated Central European commercial market with important automotive, electronics, engineering, machinery, logistics, energy, and cross-border supply-chain activity. Its small domestic scale is offset by its high export integration and close commercial links with Czechia, Austria, Germany, Poland, and Hungary.
VOLUNTARY RESOLUTION RATEComparable nationwide official figures isolating voluntary B2B collection outcomes are not consistently available. In practice, clear documentation and a credible electronic payment-order or enforcement route can strengthen negotiated settlement prospects before compulsory execution becomes necessary.
ENFORCEMENT AUTHORITY SCALESlovakia's enforcement system is structurally distinctive because enforcement petitions are centralised in one nationwide court, District Court Banská Bystrica, while assigned state-authorised enforcement agents carry out the public-power execution function. This produces a centralised intake and decentralised field-enforcement model.
CLAIM SIZE PROFILEThe market includes recurring supplier invoices, freight and logistics claims, automotive-component receivables, wholesale debt, technology-service invoices, and cross-border industrial claims. Documented monetary claims are particularly suitable for the payment-order and electronic debt-collection pathways.
TYPICAL QUESTIONS
CAN PAYMENT BE ENFORCED?Yes. Once an enforceable title exists, enforcement can proceed through an assigned court enforcement agent under the Enforcement Code. The enforcement petition is filed electronically with District Court Banská Bystrica.
CAN A SLOVAK LAWYER RECOVER THE CLAIM?Yes. Slovak lawyers can manage demand letters, settlement, payment orders, electronic debt collection, litigation, and enforcement coordination, including for foreign B2B creditors.
DOES COLLECTION REQUIRE AUTHORISATION?There is no general nationwide collection-agency licensing regime for ordinary commercial recovery. Compulsory enforcement is reserved to court enforcement agents appointed and authorised by the state.
CAN A FOREIGN CREDITOR RECOVER A DEBT IN SLOVAKIA?Yes. Foreign creditors may pursue amicable recovery, Slovak payment-order or electronic procedures, litigation, or enforcement of qualifying EU judgments and European Enforcement Orders.
WHAT IS THE TYPICAL TIMELINE?Voluntary recovery may begin immediately after default. A payment order gives the debtor 15 days to pay or defend. Contested litigation and asset enforcement take longer depending on service, objections, court workload, and the debtor's accessible assets.
WHICH AUTHORITY HANDLES ENFORCEMENT?District Court Banská Bystrica is Slovakia's nationwide enforcement court. It assigns a court enforcement agent, who carries out compulsory enforcement as an exercise of public power.
SLOVAKIA COLLECTION MODEL
SLOVAKIA MODELSlovakia combines voluntary commercial recovery, documentary payment orders, a specialised electronic debt-collection procedure, ordinary litigation for disputed claims, and a centrally filed but agent-executed enforcement system under the Enforcement Code.

Brussels I (recast), the European Enforcement Order, the European Payment Order, and the European Small Claims Procedure provide structured tools for creditors managing cross-border claims.
